## 1. Title, tagline, pillars

# DAWNWAKE
**Tagline:** *Follow the thaw. Carry what you can.*

**Theme:** *What you carry, and when you are allowed to put it down.*

**Pillars**
1. **Pilot and machine** (Titanfall 2). The relationship between Juno and Moth is the game.
2. **Scarcity with a face** (Mad Max). Every resource is a person. Water is counted in tanks, and tanks are counted in names.
3. **Quiet dread** (SOMA). The HUD is lying to you. The horror is in what is missing, not in what jumps out.
4. **Scale** (Supreme Commander 2, StarCraft 2). Gaunt is enormous, the enemy is a walking city, and L7 is a battle. The player is always one machine in something much bigger.

## 2. Premise

### 2.1 Player-facing premise (the only story text allowed outside the game)
> On Gaunt, one day lasts ten years. The dawn crawls west across the world, and the only water is the thaw at its edge. A young water-hauler, her brother, and a convoy of three hundred people dying of thirst follow it north, and the war machine she found frozen in the ice may be the only thing that can carry them there.

### 2.2 The truth in one paragraph
Juno Vey drowns in level 1. The ancient frame she climbed into is a **Cantor**, a Founders' machine built to carry the minds of the dying. It pulls her mind into its lattice and, keeping a promise she made as the water came in, tells her she survived. The "recovered fragments" of the frame's past, which the player spends at the Bench to run stronger parts, are Juno's own memories. The **Foreman**, who rules the walking mining city that drinks the thaw first, is himself a dead mind, ninety-one years old for the last fifty-five years and decaying into loops. He has hunted the frame since the moment Juno died, because a tended Cantor lattice never decays and he wants to be carried forever. The campaign ends at the pole, where the sun stands still. There Juno decides who stays in the Dredge's chair so that everyone else can finally stop walking.

## 3. Setting: Gaunt

### 3.1 World rules (do not break)
- **Gaunt** is an Earth-sized world (about 40,000 km round) that turns once every **ten Earth years**. One Gaunt day is about five years of light followed by about five years of dark.
- **The edge** (the dawn line) runs from pole to pole and crawls west at about **11 km a day at the equator**. It slows with latitude (about 7 km/day at 50°, about 2 km/day at 80°) and stands still at the poles. **This is why the Still exists:** at the pole the sun neither rises nor sets. It rests on the horizon and slides around it once every ten years. Nobody there has to walk.
- **The Dark** (west of the edge) is years of night: frozen seas, frozen ground, aurora, −60°C.
- **The Morning**, or **the band** (just east of the edge), is a strip a few hundred kilometres wide where the ice is melting. Rivers run and thaw-grass grows in days. Everything that lives follows it.
- **The Heat**, or **the Afternoon** (further east), is where the sun has stood high for years. The rivers are gone, the ground is baked to glass, the open air is 60°C or hotter, and glass-sand storms blow.
- Going north is colder, but the edge walks slower there, so each day of walking is shorter. The pole is the only place where the light stays.
- **No native animals** bigger than insects. There are reeds, lichens and thaw-grass, and **nothing flies** except machines and Kit's kites.
- **Weather** is light-driven: ice fog at the edge, dust and wind in the morning, glass storms in the Heat, aurora in the Dark, and straight-falling snow at the pole.

### 3.2 The route north
The Wake travels north-north-west, staying inside the band while it gains latitude. The Morning Count map (§9.5) shows a strip of Gaunt with the edge drawn as a glowing line and the Wake as a dot on it.

### 3.3 Factions

**The Wake: our convoy.** 40 rigs and 300 people on Day 1. It is one of maybe a dozen walking convoys left on Gaunt; most others have "gone dry". Rigs are salvaged crawlers, walkers and wheeled trains, and every one is named. There is no chief. Decisions get made around Oma Desh, because she fixes everything. The Wake burns its dead at the **Turnback fire** so the Dredge can't take them.
*Look:* patched, asymmetric salvage riveted over bevelled hulls; canvas awnings, kite ribbons, painted names; warm tungsten lights; rust, faded teal, canvas cream.

**The Dredge: the walking mining city.** It is 3 km long on forty tracked legs and rides the leading edge of the thaw, drinking first and dumping its tailings behind. About 2,000 living **Hands** (labour families paid in water) live in its water-pens. About 600 **ghosts** run its war-frames, cranes, yachts and systems; ghosts are pressed minds of the dead. It is ruled by **the Foreman** from **the Press**, the chair at the city's heart. Its outriders are **Gleaners** (scavenger drones that collect the dead for pressing), raider skiffs, land-yachts and Skimmers. Its creed is *"Work is the only prayer that gets answered."* Its broadcasts call the Still a lie: *"The Still is ice and bones."*
*Look:* monumental bevelled slabs, black iron and oxide red, hazard chevrons, sodium-orange lamps, steam. **Every ghost-run machine has a pale green cab light that blinks once a second (60 per minute).**
The Hands are not villains. They are terrified of being pressed when they die. In play, Hands' walkers surrender when staggered (L6, L8).

**The Founders (historical).** These were the colonists of **Stillwater**, the polar settlement, ninety-plus years gone. They built the Parasol, the Sundial, Carrow Station, the terraforming pylons and the Cantors. Their creed is stencilled on everything they built: **CARRY THEM HOME. SET THEM DOWN.**
*Look:* smooth white ceramic, soft chamfers, gold and cyan seam-light, rounded choir-like heads, lattice motifs.

**Cantors.** These are Founders' frames built to carry the minds of the dying home to Stillwater. Families said goodbye at **the Lay**, a ring of lattice stones, and then the Cantor "set them down" (released the mind). They were armed during the Founders' War. **Moth is Cantor 7, the last one working.**

### 3.4 Technology rules (writers must not contradict these)
- **Lattice:** the passenger space inside a Cantor. A carried mind is stored as a **self** (the person who thinks and acts) plus discrete memory **fragments**. Juno arrived as a self plus 12 fragments.
- **Kernel:** the Cantor's own mind (Moth), held in **4 kernel cells**. The kernel *tends* the lattice, and **a tended lattice does not decay.** As cells are spent, Moth's voice and personality go first; tending is the last thing to go.
- **Hungry parts:** Dredge "ghost-tech" built to be driven by a pressed mind. Running one takes lattice capacity, which means overwriting something stored there: a fragment, or (once Juno knows) a kernel cell. Plain parts are dumb machinery and cost nothing.
- **Pressing:** the Dredge's crude copy of Cantor technology (Augustin Pell, from LY 8). It traps a dead or dying mind and installs it in a machine to work. Pressed minds are **untended** and decay into **loops** (repeated phrases, fixed habits) over 5 to 15 years. The Press chair is the best press ever built and has held Pell for 55 years, but he is going.
- **Handshake:** every pressed or carried mind gives off a lattice "handshake" that Dredge gear can hear from far away. An empty Cantor is silent, so the Foreman only notices Moth once Juno is inside it.
- **Sensorium:** a lattice gives its passenger a simulated body. Moth's is old and crude. The heartbeat is locked to its idle clock (60/min), there is no heat or cold, there's a sense of eating and sleeping, and rations are "dispensed" that do not exist.
- **Recall:** the Press can try to pull any pressed or carried mind within about 1 km into itself (L6).
- **What the Foreman wants:** to evict Juno and have Moth carry *him*, so that he never decays. He cannot simply take Moth; a Cantor has to carry its passenger willingly, so he needs Moth broken or bargained with.

### 3.6 Wake idioms (use these; never explain them)
- **"Morning."** Hello, at any hour (in the Wake, it is always morning).
- **"Keep up."** Goodbye. Also the closest the Wake comes to "I love you."
- **"Gone dry" / "went dry"** means died.
- **"Sit down"** means to stop walking, and in older usage, to die. The Still is "where you sit down and get up again." The Foreman inverts it: *"Nobody gets to sit down."*
- **Cut / cutter:** to harvest ice; an ice-cutting walker.
- **The edge / the Dark / the Heat:** the dawn line, the night side, the afternoon side.
- **Tank:** the Wake's unit of water, and its unit of everything else.
- **Turnback fire:** funerals and feasts (the dead are burned so the Dredge can't press them).
- **Winter:** any stretch when the Wake has to run along the night edge to find ice ("the fever winter").
- Rigs are "she". War-frames are "it", until Kit names one.
- **The bridge song** is the Wake's lullaby, about the place where the sun sits down. Lyrics are in §12.6.

## 4. Cast

Each character has a **comms style** (the typography the comms box uses for them) and a **speed** (typewriter rate as a multiple of the default).

### JUNO VEY: pilot (the player character)
- **Who:** 24, a water-hauler. She has raised her brother Kit since their father died four years ago. Clipped, dry and competent, funny in a deadpan way. She shows love by giving orders and by keeping track of other people's water. She never talks about herself.
- **Flaw:** she cannot put anything down.
- **Voice rules:** short declaratives and imperatives, rarely more than 12 words. No exclamation marks (the only exception is the moment she surfaces in L1). She deflects feelings with logistics ("Drink something."). She calls Kit "Kit", or "kid" when she's scared. Mild swearing only ("dry it", "hell"). When something hurts, she goes quieter, not louder.
- **Comms style:** amber, sentence case. Speed 1.0.
- **Arc:**
  - L1–L2: carries everyone.
  - L3–L5: trusts Moth, and forgets things without knowing she has forgotten them.
  - L6: learns she is dead, and says nothing for the whole escape.
  - L7: keeps going anyway (*"Water first. Then we argue about what I am."*, a callback to her L1 line), and lets other people carry her (Retelling, Oma's gate).
  - L8: decides what to put down.
- **Samples:** *"Water first. Then we argue about what you are."* · *"Kit, fly that kite over the ridge once more and I tie it to your ankle."* · *"Big Mercy, keep your wheels straight. Don't watch the harpoons. Watch me."*

### MOTH: Cantor 7 (the frame's mind)
- **Who:** an ancient Founders' Cantor with no memory of itself. Formal, literal and curious. It learns to be a person by watching Juno and logging what it sees. Kit names it in L1 because when it idles it always turns north, "like a moth at a lamp." Its love is real. So is its lie.
- **Voice rules:** no contractions. It uses verbal tags: "Query:", "Logged.", "Correction:", "Noted." It reports feelings as data at first, and "I think" first appears in L3. **It calls itself "this frame" in L1 right up to the drowning, where it says "me" for the first time ("Hold on to me.").** From then on it says "I". It is the moment Moth becomes someone, and it is the same moment Juno dies.
- **The honesty rule:** Moth tells exactly **three direct lies** in the whole game:
  1. "You are okay." (L1)
  2. "Recovered during the thaw. They are mine, I think." (Bench 1)
  3. "The hatch seal is damaged." (Bench 1, L5)

  Everything else Moth says about Juno's state is literally true or an evasion ("She is resting."). The post-credits Lattice Log annotates all of them (§8.5).
- **Kernel degradation** (after L6, by cells left):
  - 4 cells: normal.
  - 3 cells: no queries, no curiosity, statements only.
  - 2 cells: six words or fewer per line, and never "I".
  - 1 cell: single words.
  - 0 cells: silent; its comms slot shows "…".
  - **Every scripted Moth line from Bench 6 on is written in five versions (4 cells down to 0).** Write the 4-cell line first, then compress. Example: 4: *"He is fighting a war that ended a hundred and six years ago."* · 3: *"He is fighting a war that ended long ago."* · 2: *"Old war. He is looping."* · 1: *"Looping."* · 0: *"…"*
- **Comms style:** cyan, monospace small caps, no radio static (it's on the local channel). Speed 1.0.
- **Arc:**
  - L1: machine.
  - L2–L5: becomes a person, by watching her.
  - L6: confession.
  - L7: recovers its own past (Corran) and understands why it lied.
  - L8: decides whether loving her means keeping her or setting her down.
- **Samples:** *"Query: you said 'it's fine' with a falling pitch. My records indicate that means it is not fine."* · *"Query: when Kit laughs twice after saying he is fine, is that a second data point, or the same one?"*

### KIT VEY: scout, 15
- **Who:** Juno's brother. Loud, funny and recklessly brave. He hero-worships Juno and pretends he doesn't. He drives the buggy **Tick** and flies a kite-drone. He jokes when he is scared.
- **Voice rules:** run-ons, dashes, self-interruptions, "okay so", the occasional ALL-CAPS word, kite puns. He calls her "Jun" and calls Moth "big guy". He is never cruel. When he is truly frightened he goes short and flat, the only time he sounds like Juno.
- **Comms style:** yellow-green, light radio static. Speed 1.4.
- **Arc:**
  - L1–L5: the kid who needs carrying.
  - End of L5: captured.
  - L6: hears the truth over the open channel.
  - Bench 6 and L7: refuses to let it change the count ("Two souls.") and becomes the keeper of her memories (Retelling).
  - L8: carries her.
- **Samples:** *"Kite's up! Good news: I can see the Dredge. Bad news: I can see the Dredge."* · *"Okay so the dam's bigger than I said. Like, a lot bigger. Kite's fine though! Kite's great!"*

### OMA DESH: elder and mechanic, 72
- **Who:** runs **the Bench**, the Wake's workshop crawler and the game's garage. Gruff and thrifty, never sentimental out loud; everything she says is about parts. **She has known the truth since the first night.** She opened the hatch at Bench 1 and found the cockpit empty and dry. She kept the secret because the Wake needed the frame and Kit needed his sister.
- **Voice rules:** short, weighty sentences and salvage proverbs. She calls Juno "girl", Kit "boy", and Moth "the frame" (until Bench 6, when she calls it "Moth" for the first time). She never asks a question she doesn't already know the answer to.
- **Comms style:** warm white, slightly heavier weight. Speed 0.8.
- **Arc:** complicit guardian, then confession at Bench 6, then she holds the Gate in L7. She lives or dies depending on how well the Wake was armed (§7.10).
- **Samples:** *"Everything on this rig was something else first. That's not salvage, girl. That's family history."* · *"You can have the condenser or the cannon. Not both, not neither. Pick."*

### THE FOREMAN: Augustin Pell (antagonist)
- **Who:** the Founders' Works Foreman. He built the Dredge, invented pressing, and had himself pressed at 91, fifty-five years ago. He is decaying into loops.
- **Voice rules:** heard only on the **OPEN** channel, always preceded by a descending three-note chime. Patient, reasonable and fatherly; he never shouts. He uses labour metaphors and calls Juno **"little rig."** He **repeats lines VERBATIM** (list in §12.1). From L6 onward his loops break through: sentences restart, and in L7 he calls Juno "Corran" and Moth "Seven".
- **Comms style:** pale gold, calm italics, slowest typing. Speed 0.7.
- **Arc:** tyrant's voice, then an exposed loop (L7), then, in the Press, a tired old man who never let himself sit down (L8).

### TALLOW: Dredge ace, pressed (real name Tam)
- **Who:** a hauler from the convoy *Lantern Line*, pressed eight years ago. She is fraying into loops: sing-song, childlike, unsettling at first and then sad.
- **Voice rules:** doubled words ("hello hello"), half-finished sentences, sensory non sequiturs. **She speaks on Juno's LOCAL channel, mind to mind, with no radio static, which no living speaker could do.** That is a clue.
- **Comms style:** pale green; letters jitter as they type. Speed irregular.
- **Arc:**
  - L2–L3: a watcher on the ridges who never fires.
  - L4: the duel, and the player's choice.
  - L7: if spared, refuses the Foreman.
  - L8: if spared, fights beside Juno and remembers her name.
- **Samples:** *"Hello hello. You smell like me. Like cold. Hello hello."* · *"I had a name. It was a short one. It was a— it was a short one."*

### TALLYMAN RUSK: Dredge water-officer (alive until L3)
- **Who:** commands the harpoon fleet (L2) and the Gerrow Dam (L3). A swaggering bookkeeper who talks in tallies, debts and overdue accounts, and is secretly terrified of being pressed. He drowns in L3 and is pressed, and in L6 he works a conveyor as a ghost, looping his own boasts.
- **Comms style:** oxide red, quick (1.2), heavy static.
- **Samples:** *"Every drop on Gaunt is tallied, hauler. Yours are overdue."* · *"Your frame's handshake is loud. We can hear you from the Dredge."*

### Minor voices (Wake radio: grey, with the rig name as a prefix)
- **Hesper Lull** (*Compass Rose*): navigator, in her sixties. Precise; she speaks in bearings and steers by the Sundial's shadow.
- **Ansel Brook** (*Big Mercy*): driver of the water tanker. Big, gentle and superstitious, and hums on an open mic.
- **Dree and Dov Pruitt** (*Twice Shy*): twin gunners who finish each other's sentences. They crew the Mortar Rig Call (L3 onward) and the L7 mortars.
- **Ada Corran:** Stillwater's commander, in recordings only, in L7. Clean serif text with timestamps.
- **Ilan Vey** (their father) never speaks. He exists only in fragments and in the L6 registry.

## 5. The twists

### 5.1 Twist 1: Juno died on the Rime Shelf
**What happened.** In L1 the floe breaks and Moth sinks. The cockpit floods. As the water reaches her, Juno makes Moth promise: *"Don't let me end up one of his ghosts."* Then she drowns. Moth is a Cantor and does what it was built for: it carries her, pulling her mind into its lattice. Her vitals flatline for four seconds. When the lattice takes her, the pilot-vitals widget starts reporting Moth's sensorium, which reads 60 bpm forever. Moth tells her she's okay. At the shore, it kneels at a hole in the ice and lets her body go into the sea ("Releasing ballast.").

**Why Moth lied.** Newly woken and with no past of its own, Moth heard "don't let me become a ghost" and could see only one way to keep that promise after it had already carried her: she must never know. In L7, Moth finds the deeper reason. It once lost a passenger (Ada Corran) to a century of ice and would not lose another. Moth carried Juno but never *set her down*, and that is its sin.

**What the fragments are.** Juno came into the lattice as her self plus 12 fragments. Moth labelled the fragments with machine glosses and called them its own recovered memories. **Every fragment overwritten at the Bench is permanently gone from Juno.** She doesn't feel the loss. She just doesn't have the memory any more, and someone else mentions it later (§7.7, "echo").

**Who knows, and when.**
- **Moth:** always.
- **Oma:** from Bench 1 (the empty hatch).
- **The Foreman:** from the end of L1 (he heard the handshake).
- **Tallow:** senses it ("You smell like me").
- **Kit:** not until L6, when the Foreman broadcasts it.

**The reveal (L6, in the Press).** The Foreman forces Moth's lattice onto Juno's HUD. It is the same screen as the Bench's lattice tab, but now in hostile hands. The header retypes letter by letter from `MOTH — RECOVERED FRAGMENTS` to `JUNO VEY`. Each card retitles in her own words, and overwritten cards show their titles struck through. Then the registry appears:
`VEY, JUNO — CARRIED: WALK DAY 1 — CARRIER: CANTOR 7`
`VEY, ILAN — PRESSED: LY 112 — STATUS: EXPENDED`
Moth confesses (full script in §12.2).

### 5.2 Twist 2: the Foreman is a ghost
Augustin Pell died at 91 in LY 61 and has run the Dredge from the Press chair for 55 years. His famous patience is literal repetition. He has hunted Moth since L1 because a tended Cantor lattice never decays: he wants to evict Juno and be carried forever.
- **Revealed in L7.** He re-fights the Founders' War beat for beat (the Script, §6 L7), and his loops break open on the channel.
- **Confirmed in L8,** inside the Press.

### 5.3 Revelation: Moth's past (L7)
- Moth is **Cantor 7**. It carried **41** people home to Stillwater and set each of them down.
- In the war it carried the dying mind of **Commander Ada Corran** off Founders' Field and fled south into the Dark so Pell could not press her.
- It froze into the Rime Shelf. Across 106 years of cold its kernel lost itself, and Corran, untended, was gone.
- The dead Cantors at Founders' Field kept a copy of Moth in their shared mesh, the **choir**. When Moth walks among them, it remembers.
- Moth's line: *"I was carrying her into the dark. The ice was very long. When I woke there was no one in the lattice. I was not awake to hold her."*

## 6. The campaign: eight levels

### 6.0 Rules for every level
- **One continuous streamed map per level** (chunked heightfield along a route spline), crossed from start to end, 2.5 to 9 km long. You never return to an earlier level's map.
- **Opening vista:** every level opens on a 6 to 10 s letterboxed, in-engine shot of its **hero landmark**, which is where the level ends. Skippable.
- **Structure:** 4 to 6 checkpoints, 20 to 35 minutes, one **Bench** visit afterwards (§7), one **Morning Count** card (§9.5).
- **Each level has** its own sun, its own hero landmark, one new enemy family, one gameplay twist, and one signature set piece.
- **Scale tiers** for battle levels (L2, L7, L8): *near* units with full AI; *mid* units that are instanced with simple AI; and a *far* "battle painting" of silhouettes on splines, with tracer and artillery arcs. Low tier thins the outer two tiers.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | THAW: The Rime Shelf | Frozen sea; night, then sunrise | Systems "remembered" mid-fight; sunlit ice rots | Raider skiffs, Gleaners, the Icebreaker | Floe collapse, drowning, floe sprint |

### LEVEL 1: THAW (The Rime Shelf)
*The first level built. The most detailed.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location and biome** | A frozen sea at the night edge: pressure ridges, ice caves, blue-black ice under stars, with a thin red dawn rim to the east. The colony freighter *Abeyance* is frozen upright in the ice, 140 m tall. Ice fog. |
| **Light** | Starlight and aurora at the start. When the sun clears the Thornback ridge (scripted, minute 15), a wall of red-gold light sweeps across the shelf. |

| **New enemies** | **Raider skiffs** (wind-and-turbine ice yachts with harpoons and gunners). **Gleaners** (Dredge scavenger drones with green lights; a large carrier variant). **The Icebreaker** (a 60 m Dredge drill-crawler). |

**Beats**
1. **Prologue card** (text on black, typewriter): *"GAUNT. One day lasts ten years. The dawn walks west, eleven kilometres a day. Behind it, the ground burns. Ahead of it, the sea is ice. Between them, for a few hundred kilometres, there is water. We follow it."*
2. **The cutter (2 minutes).** Juno pilots her ice-cutter, a small four-legged utility walker with a saw arm (built with the plate kit, weak, unarmed). She cuts three ice blocks onto a sled; this teaches movement, camera and the saw on the blade input. Kit chatters from his kite overhead. Kit: *"Lights on the ice. Lots of lights. Jun, those aren't ours."*
3. **The raid.** Raider skiffs harpoon the sled and rake the cutter. The cutter breaks through a crevasse roof and falls into an ice cavern. Fade to black, and a text card: *"Its hatch was open. It was the only warm thing in the dark."*
4. **Waking Moth.** First-person boot text over black:
   `CANTOR 7 // KERNEL 4/4 // LATTICE: EMPTY // PASSENGER: NONE // PILOT: DETECTED`
   Moth: *"Query: are you authorised?"* Juno: *"I'm the only one here, so yes."* Moth: *"This frame accepts."* The camera pulls out to third person: a white ceramic frame with ice scars and rust streaks, its cyan visor lighting up.
5. **Walk only.** Moth's systems are dark. A short cavern walk ends at a shaft. Moth: *"Boost system… remembered."* Boost unlocks, and the player boosts up the shaft into starlight.
6. **The ridges.** Kit's buggy *Tick* is pinned by two skiffs. Moth: *"Targeting… remembered."* Lock-on and rifle unlock. **TEAR tutorial:** a skiff is staggered, the TEAR prompt appears, and Moth rips its **Gaff Harpoon** off. The Haul rack UI appears: *"HAUL 1/3. Parts go to the Bench at the end of the walk."*
7. **Gleaners swarm** the dead skiffs, and one closes in. Moth: *"Blade… remembered."* The blade unlocks. Kit names nothing yet; he's busy screaming encouragement.
8. **The *Abeyance*.** Kit drives through the freighter's hold while Juno climbs its broken hull inside (vertical boosting through decks). On a bulkhead is a Founders' stencil: a lattice-ring emblem matching the one on Moth's shoulder, and the words **CARRY THEM HOME. SET THEM DOWN.** Moth stops by itself for 2 seconds: *"This frame knows this mark."* Juno: *"From where?"* Moth: *"Unknown. Logged."*
9. **The cut-line.** From the freighter's top deck: the Icebreaker, a Dredge drill-crawler, is sawing the shelf into floes to harvest, guarded by skiffs. Kit: *"That's the ice we came for. They're taking all of it."* **Fight:** destroy its three drill heads (weak points) while it crawls and skiffs harass.
10. **Sunrise (scripted).** The sun clears the Thornback ridge, and a wall of red-gold light runs across the shelf toward the player. Where it touches, the ice groans. The Icebreaker's last drill head breaks loose and the floe under Moth splits.
11. **The drowning (set piece, about 40 s, no control for the last 12).** Moth falls through into dark green water, with bubbles and silt. HUD: `CABIN BREACH`. A water line rises up the HUD glass, and the vitals widget spikes and races.
    - Juno: *"Moth— Moth, listen. If this goes bad. Don't let me end up one of his ghosts. Promise me."*
    - Moth: *"Promised."*
    - The vitals go flat. `SENSOR FAULT`. The screen cuts to black for **4 seconds** with only Moth's text: *"Hold on to me."*
    - The HUD reboots. Vitals read **60**.
    - Juno: *"…I'm okay? I'm okay!"* (her only exclamation mark in the game)
    - Moth: *"You are okay."* (Lie #1.) Boost up through the hole.
12. **The floe sprint (3 minutes).** **Gameplay twist:** sunlit ice rots. Any ice in direct sun cracks white and collapses about 3 s after Moth lands on it, while shadowed blue ice holds. Leap floe to floe east toward the shore, where Kit is lighting flares, with skiffs harassing.

15. **The name.** At rest, the frame slowly turns to face north. Kit: *"It keeps turning north. Like a moth at a lamp. I'm calling it Moth."* Moth: *"Designation accepted. Logged."* Fade.

- **Signature set piece:** the sunrise, the collapse, the drowning, and the floe sprint.
- **Boss / climax:** the Icebreaker (three drill heads), then the drowning and the sprint.
- **How it ends:** Moth reaches the Wake and gets its name. Morning Count, Day 1.
- **Low tier:** stars and aurora on the skybox texture; floes as instanced bevelled slabs; underwater done with fog and a tint; the *Abeyance* as one merged mesh.

### LEVEL 5: THE LONG NIGHT (Carrow Station)

## 7. THE BENCH: the garage mechanic

### 7.1 The rule in one line
**Rip parts off staggered enemies. At the Bench, fit them, keep them, or give them to the Wake for water. But the best parts are *hungry*, and every hungry part you run permanently overwrites one of the frame's "recovered fragments" (and the player doesn't yet know those fragments are Juno's memories).**

### 7.2 Why it exists in the world
- The Wake has no money. Water is the only thing of value, and you drink it rather than trade it. Every part is salvage, and **the Bench** is Oma's tracked workshop crawler: crane arms, a welding lamp, a kneeling cradle for the frame.
- Plain Dredge parts are dumb machinery that Oma can bolt on.
- **Hungry** parts are Dredge ghost-tech, built to be driven by a pressed mind. They only run if something in Moth's lattice is cleared to make room.
- Moth says the lattice is full of its own recovered past, and that clearing it costs nothing that matters. That is the lie the whole mechanic rests on.
- Giving a part away makes the Wake stronger: Oma turns it into condensers, pump seals and tanker plate, which means more water and more armour on the rigs.

### 7.3 Resources at a glance

| Resource | What it is | Gained by | Spent on / affects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Haul** | Parts torn off enemies, carried in a 3-slot rack | TEAR on staggered enemies; caches; story gifts | Arrives at the Bench after the level |
| **Water** (tanks) | The Wake's survival | Level objectives and caches; **Give** | The draw at every Morning Count; **Calls**; **Retelling** |
| **Arms** | Total value of everything ever given | **Give** (equal to the part's give value) | The L7 Gate, mortars and kites; Oma's survival |
| **Fragments** | 12 of Juno's memories, plus up to 5 made during play | Made fragments arrive after L2, L3, L4, L5 and L7; Retelling restores half | Paying hunger; the **Sync** meter size; L8 shield pips; ending gates |
| **Kernel** | Moth's own self: 4 cells (usable after the reveal) | Never regained | Paying hunger instead of a fragment; Moth's voice; ending gate |
| **Sync** | Combat meter; segments = fragment value | Dealing damage, perfect dodges, TEARs | Partner moves: Second Sight, Moth Drives, Hold |

### 7.4 Hardpoints and the stock loadout
Moth has **six hardpoints**. Stock parts belong to Moth: they are always owned, never hungry, and can't be given away. They match the prototype's existing values.

| Hardpoint | Controls (desktop / touch) | Stock part | Stock behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| R-ARM | LMB / FIRE | **Lantern Carbine** | Balanced auto-rifle, 600 rounds (prototype HR-30) |
| L-ARM | RMB / BLADE | **Psalter Blade** | Pulse-blade lunge (prototype PB-2) |
| SHOULDER | Q / MSL | **Chorus Rack** | 4-lock missiles, 80 (prototype VM-4) |
| BOOSTER | Space, Shift / BOOST, QB | **Cantor Wings** | Boost, hover, 2 quick-boost charges |
| LEGS | (movement) | **Cantor Stride** | Biped: jump, hover, balanced |
| CORE | (passive) | **Hymnal Core** | AP 9000 · EN 1000 · stability (impact max) 1400 |

Utility (R, repair kits ×3) is not a hardpoint and never changes.

### 7.5 Haul: tear-offs and the rack
- Any enemy carrying a haul part shows a **glint** on that part: amber for plain, a pale green-white lattice shimmer for hungry.
- When that enemy is **staggered** and within 30 m, the melee input shows **TEAR**. Hold it for 0.4 s. Moth lunges, grabs and rips in a 1.2 s animation with a camera punch-in, invulnerable throughout. TEAR works whatever is in the L-ARM slot.
- **Result:** small and medium enemies are destroyed. Heavies and bosses lose that part *and its function* (for example, a Harpooner that loses its harpoon stops harpooning). Each TEAR gives **+2 Sync segments**.
- **Rack:** 3 slots. When the rack is full, a new TEAR asks you to **swap**; the dropped part stays as a pickup where you dropped it until the level ends.
- Some parts come from **caches** (pickups) or **story** (Oma's charges in L3, the dead Cantors in L7).
- At the end of a level the whole rack goes to the Bench. **Duplicates** of a part you already own become **spares**, which can only be given away.

### 7.6 Sync, partner moves and shield pips
- **Segments** equal fragment value rounded down. Intact fragments count 1 and Retold fragments count 0.5. With 12 intact fragments you have 12 segments; with 5, you have 5. Every overwrite visibly removes a segment from the meter.
- **Filling (tune):** +1 per 1,500 damage dealt, +1 per perfect dodge (a quick-boost within 0.25 s of a hit that would have landed), and +2 per TEAR. The meter doesn't drain.
- **Unlocked in L2.** **Inputs (suggested):** on desktop, **E** for Second Sight and **F** for Moth Drives (near a dead Cantor in L7, F becomes **SING**). On touch, a single **SYNC** button opens a two-option radial. Hold has no input.

| Move | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Second Sight** | 3 | 6 s: time slows to 70%, every incoming projectile shows its path, and enemy wind-ups flash. |
| **Moth Drives** | 4 | 8 s: Moth takes over movement, dodging with free quick-boosts and keeping the best range for your weapons, while you aim and fire. |
| **Hold** | 6 (4 with Cantor Mantle) | Automatic. When a hit would take AP to 0, Hold spends the segments, leaves 1 AP and gives 2 s of invulnerability. Moth: *"Hold on to me."* |

- **When Hold becomes impossible.** If fragment value drops below Hold's cost, Hold can never trigger. The widget shows HOLD greyed out. The first time this happens Moth speaks at the Bench, pre-reveal: *"I do not have enough context to hold you."* Post-reveal: *"There is not enough of you left for me to hold."*
- **At 0 kernel cells**, partner moves fire automatically: Second Sight on any missile lock (if 3 segments are filled), Moth Drives when AP drops below 25% (if 4 are filled). Hold is unchanged.
- **L8 lattice-space:** there is no Sync. Juno gets **shield pips** equal to fragment value rounded down, **plus 1** (her self). Each pip absorbs one hit.

### 7.8 Bench actions: exact rules
1. **States.** Every owned part is **FITTED**, in the **LOCKER**, or **GIVEN** (gone for good). The Locker holds **4** parts (**5** from Bench 4).
2. **Fit.** Put a part in its hardpoint. The part it replaces goes to the Locker, or must be given if the Locker is full. Plain parts cost nothing.
3. **Hunger.** The *first* time a hungry part is fitted, pay its hunger: overwrite that many fragments (the player picks which; Moth highlights its recommendation). The part then becomes **BOUND**, and refitting it is free forever. Overwriting only ever happens to pay hunger.
4. **Give.** Strip a part for the Wake: **Water += give value; Arms += give value.** This is permanent. Giving away a bound part does **not** restore its fragments.
5. **Fund Calls** (from Bench 2): pay tanks for the next level's Calls (§7.9).
6. **Undo.** Any action can be undone until the player presses **WALK ON**, *except* overwrites, which confirm when made:
   - Before the reveal: a single tap, with the gloss shown.
   - After the reveal: typed (see 8).
7. **Bench 5 is rushed:** at most **2 actions**, where Fit, Give and Fund each count as one. Un-fitting is free.
8. **After the reveal (Bench 6 and 7):**
   - **Kernel.** Any hunger point may be paid with one of Moth's 4 kernel cells instead of a fragment. Moth offers this the first time an overwrite prompt appears: *"Take me instead."* Each spent cell degrades Moth's voice (§4). This cannot be undone.
   - **Typed confirmation.** To overwrite a fragment, type the key word of its true title (e.g. `PATIENCE`), shown greyed underneath. On touch, press and hold for 3 s while Moth spells the title out letter by letter on comms.
   - **Retelling.** Each *living* teller (Kit, Oma) may retell one overwritten fragment per Bench visit for **2 tanks** each ("the Wake stops for a night"). The fragment returns as **RETOLD** (value 0.5). Moth can retell M3 or M5 for **1 kernel cell** (no water).
9. **Post-level order:** debrief → **Bench** → **Morning Count** (the water draw is applied) → next level's vista. L5 is the exception: the Count card shows first, then the rushed Bench.

### 8.2 The Chair: rule and gates
**Rule:** the Dredge's dead-man loop cannot be stopped from outside. A mind in the chair can **steer** it, **sink** it, or **end** it. Ending it (the Release) frees every pressed mind aboard, including the mind that does it.

The three options are always *shown*. A locked option is greyed out with a reason, never hidden:

| Option | Requires | If locked, it reads |
|---|---|---|
| **"I'll stay."** | Fragment value ≥ 4 (enough of Juno to hold the chair without looping) | *"Not enough of you to stay."* |
| **"Moth stays."** | Kernel ≥ 1 | *"Moth is silent."* |
| **"Nobody stays."** | Always available | — |

### 8.3 The three endings (all bittersweet)

**ENDING 1: "I'll stay."** *Juno steers.*
- **Scene:** Juno sits. The Dredge stops at the edge of Stillwater's harbour with one leg in the water. Its flare-stacks go out one by one, and its sodium lamps turn warm white. Its pumps reverse and begin to heat Stillwater's domes.
- Moth lets go of her tether and sends her remaining fragments up the link into the chair. Then, **empty**, it walks down the listing deck and onto the ice, to where the *Tick* waits. It kneels, and for the first time in the game the hatch opens on screen. Moth: *"Morning, Kit."*
- **Epilogue card:** *"The Dredge never walked again. The Wake calls it the Chair. Every morning (and it is always morning) a voice on the city horns reads the Wake Roll, name by name."*
  - Fragment value ≥ 8: *"She never misses one."*
  - Fragment value 4 to 7: *"Some years she misses a name. Kit stands under the horn and says it for her."*
- **Bridge song:**
  - F02 intact: *"At the end of the Roll, she sings."*
  - F02 retold: *"She sings the version Kit taught her back."*
  - F02 overwritten: *"Kit sings it up to her. She says she likes it. She says it every time like it's new."*

**ENDING 2: "Moth stays."** *Moth sinks it.*
- **Scene:** Juno comes back down the link into the frame. Moth leaves the frame for the chair. The frame's visor shifts from cyan to amber, and the HUD's Moth widget goes dark. Juno is alone in the frame for the first time.
- **Moth's last line, by kernel cells left:**
  - 4: *"I carried you as far as I could. Now you carry them. Set them down when you are ready, and not before."*
  - 3: *"You carry them now. Set them down when you are ready."*
  - 2: *"Carry them. Then rest."*
  - 1: *"Go."*
- The Dredge turns from the harbour and walks out into the deep polar sea, its lights going under one by one. Juno walks the frame to Stillwater's shore and leads the Wake in.
- **Epilogue card:** *"Without a Cantor to tend her, she will loop, the way Tallow looped. Slowly. Years. Kit knows. Every night he reads her the fragments, the ones she has and the ones she doesn't, from a book he wrote by hand."*
- **Bridge song:**
  - F02 intact: *"She sings him to sleep. Same song. Same flat note."*
  - F02 lost or retold: *"He sings it to her. She hums along, a beat behind."*

**ENDING 3: "Nobody stays."** *They end it together.*
- **Scene:** Juno and Moth perform the Release. If Moth has 0 cells it shows only "…", and Juno says: *"I know. Me too."*
- Every cab light on the Dredge, hundreds of green lights, turns white and rises as motes into the twilight. Lights from any released Carrow banks are already waiting at the Lay.
- **Tallow** (if spared): *"Tam. It was Tam. Goodbye goodbye."*
- The Dredge stops at the harbour's edge. On the shore the frame faces north and kneels, and its visor goes out.
- **Juno's last line:**
  - F12 intact or retold: *"Never us, Kit. Remember? This isn't them taking me. I'm setting myself down. That's different."*
  - F12 overwritten: *"Keep up, Kit."*
- **Then the song:**
  - F02 intact: she sings the last line herself, *"and we'll sit down too, in the quiet town."*
  - Otherwise, Kit sings it to her over the radio as the light goes.
- Kit's buggy pulls up. He ties his kite to the frame's shoulder. There is no wind at the Still, so the kite rests there, folded, like a moth.
- **Epilogue card:** *"The Wake stopped walking on Day 131. CARRY THEM HOME. SET THEM DOWN."*

**Every ending then shows the Roll card:** *"[N] souls reached the Still."* The names lost along the way are listed in grey, and then the names that made it, in white.
- If Oma lived, the last line is hers: *"Bench is shut. First time in sixty years. Don't anyone break anything."*
- If she didn't, Kit adds her name to the top of the grey list by hand.

### 8.4 Epilogue variables (state that colours every ending)
- Wake Roll: souls and named rigs that reach the Still.
- F02 (bridge song) and F12 (Dad): intact, retold or overwritten.
- Fragment value (Ending 1 detail) and kernel cells (Ending 2 line).
- Oma alive; Tallow torn or spared; Carrow banks released; Hands' rigs joined; *Big Mercy* saved.

### 8.5 After the credits: the Lattice Log (graft from Pitch B)
An unlockable screen in Moth's full voice, "recovered from the Cantor choir", whatever happened to its kernel. It lists every lie and evasion Moth told, each annotated with the truth. For example:
- *"She is resting."* → `SHE WAS IN ME. SHE WAS NOT RESTING. I DID NOT HAVE A BETTER WORD.`
- *"Releasing ballast."* → `IT WAS HER. I PUT HER IN THE SEA BECAUSE I DID NOT KNOW WHAT ELSE A CANTOR DOES WITHOUT A LAY.`
- *"The hatch seal is damaged."* → `IT OPENS. IT ALWAYS OPENED.`

It also lists every fragment the player overwrote, each with its echo line and the date it was lost. This is the only place the full annotated story ever appears.

## 10. Tone and comms style guide

**Tone:** dry, warm and sparse. Grief carried under logistics, and humour as a survival skill. Nobody makes speeches. It should feel like people too thirsty to waste words, who still make each other laugh.

**Rules**
1. **Length:** in combat, at most 80 characters per line and 2 lines per beat. Quiet scenes may run up to 4 lines.
2. **Subtext beats statement.** People talk about water, parts and routes, and feelings leak out through the logistics. If a line says the feeling out loud, cut it.
3. **Voice test:** every line must be recognisable as its speaker with the name hidden (§4 voice rules).
4. **One fact, once.** A fact about the world appears in comms at most once; after that it lives in the environment. No exposition dumps.
5. **Every level must have jokes,** and they come from character: Kit's mouth, Juno's deadpan, Moth's literalism, Oma's thrift.
6. **Protect the twist:** obey §5.5 and Moth's three-lie rule.
7. **Use Wake idioms and never explain them** (§3.6).
8. **Silence is a line.** Scripted silences are written into the level scripts like dialogue.
9. **At most two speakers per exchange in combat.** The Foreman's chime is the only interruption allowed.
10. **Profanity** goes no harder than "dry it" or "hell".
11. **Spelling:** British (colour, kilometre, apologise).
12. **VERBATIM lines** are copy-pasted from §12.1. Never retype them from memory.

**Sample lines**
1. JUNO (L2): *"Big Mercy, keep your wheels straight. Don't watch the harpoons. Watch me."*
2. MOTH (L3): *"Query: when Kit laughs twice after saying he is fine, is that a second data point, or the same one?"*
3. KIT (L3): *"Okay so the dam's bigger than I said. Like, a lot bigger. Kite's fine though! Kite's great!"*
4. OMA (Bench 3): *"Hungry part. Feeds off the frame's head. Don't ask me how; I just bolt them on."*
5. FOREMAN (V4): *"I am ninety-one years old, little rig, and I have never once sat down."*
6. TALLOW (L4): *"Hello hello. Sun's loud today. You're quiet. You're quiet like me."*
7. RUSK (L2): *"Every drop on Gaunt is tallied, hauler. Yours are overdue."*
8. MOTH (Bench 6): *"Take me instead. There are four of me. There are fewer of you than there should be."*
9. JUNO (L7): *"Water first. Then we argue about what I am."*
10. KIT (Bench 6): *"I don't care what the lattice says. Second Patience. Two souls. I counted."*

## 11. Visual language (the bar is the prototype's bevelled mechs)

- **Everything is built from the bevelled plate kit** (`plateGeo` / `acKit` style extruded profiles). No raw boxes should be visible anywhere: rigs, the Dredge, landmarks and the Founders' architecture are all bevelled profiles. Design silhouettes first.
- **Moth:** based on the Vanguard frame, recoloured as weathered white ceramic with rust streaks and ice scars, a cyan visor, and the lattice-ring emblem on the left shoulder. Fitted parts change its silhouette (§7.12).
- **Tallow:** Striker frame, in Dredge colours, scarred, with a green cab light. **The Foreman's frame:** Bastion frame scaled ×2, in black iron and gold.
- **The Wake:** warm, patched and asymmetric, with canvas, kites and painted names.
- **The Dredge:** monumental slabs, oxide red, sodium orange, hazard chevrons, steam.
- **The Founders:** white ceramic, soft chamfers, gold and cyan seams.
- **Ghosts:** a green cab light blinking at 60 a minute. That is their whole tell, so make it readable at 1 km.
- **Light is the art budget.** One sun per level:

| Level | Key light | Palette |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Starlight, then a red-gold sunrise wall | Blue-black ice, red rim |
| 2 | Low gold morning | Red-gold reeds, dust |
| 3 | Hard morning sun, black shadow | Red rock, white sky slots, sodium dam lamps |
| 4 | White overhead | Bleached glass, mirror glints |
| 5 | None (aurora, flares, green lights) | Black, green, teal |
| 6 | Split: gold tail, sodium middle, starlit bow | Orange and steam against night |
| 7 | Pale low blue | White giants, rime, ice fog |
| 8 | Fixed gold twilight | Gold snow, blue shadow, white domes |

- **Low tier:** impostor landmarks, a shorter draw distance, no shadows, fewer particles, instanced crowds at low LOD, and lighting that is as cheap as possible (one directional light plus a few points).

### 12.8 Glossary
**Band / Morning:** the habitable thaw strip. **Bench:** Oma's workshop crawler; the garage. **Bound:** a hungry part whose hunger has been paid. **Cantor:** a Founders' mind-carrying frame. **Chair:** the Press seat. **Edge:** the dawn line. **Fragment:** one stored memory. **Ghost:** a pressed mind. **Gleaner:** a Dredge corpse-collecting drone. **Hands:** the Dredge's living labour. **Handshake:** the signal a mind in a lattice gives off. **Hungry:** a ghost-tech part that needs lattice room. **Kernel:** Moth's own mind (4 cells). **Lattice:** the passenger space. **Lay:** Stillwater's ring of stones, where minds were set down. **Press:** the Dredge's chair-core. **Retold:** a memory restored in someone else's words (half value). **Set down:** to release a carried mind. **Sit down:** to stop walking; to die. **Still:** the pole. **Stillwater:** the Founders' city there. **Tank:** a unit of water. **Wake:** our convoy. **Wake Roll:** the list of rigs and souls.
