# DAWNWAKE — Pitch A: Pilot and Machine

> **Build team only. Full spoilers. Never show the player.**

**Logline:** On Gaunt, where one day lasts ten years and the only water is the thaw at the edge of the moving dawn, a young water-hauler and the amnesiac war machine that pulled her from the ice must carry a starving convoy to the one place on the planet where people can finally stop walking.

---

## The World

Gaunt turns so slowly that the dawn line crawls west about eleven kilometres a day. Behind it the afternoon bakes the land to glass, and ahead of it the night is frozen. Life exists only in the morning band, where the ice melts. Convoys follow the thaw forever, but the Foreman's mining city, **the Dredge**, rides the front edge and drinks the melt first. Its war-frames are crewed by **ghosts**: dead people "pressed" into machine minds so they can keep working. Everyone fears ending up as one. Our convoy, **the Wake** (forty rigs, three hundred people), is dying of thirst behind it. Their hope is a rumour: **the Still**, the polar twilight where the sun never moves and nobody has to walk.

**Theme:** what you carry, and when you are allowed to put it down.

---

## Cast

**JUNO VEY** (pilot, the player). A 24-year-old water-hauler, clipped and dry. She shows love by giving orders.
> "Water first. Then we argue about what you are."
> "Kit, fly that kite over the ridge once more and I tie it to your ankle."

**MOTH** (the war-frame's mind). An ancient, amnesiac war-frame found in the night-ice. Formal and literal, it logs feelings as data and learns to be a person by watching Juno. Kit names it Moth because it always turns north when idle, "like a moth at a lamp."
> "Query: you said 'it's fine' with a falling pitch. My records indicate that means it is not fine."
> "Fragment overwritten. It was rain, I think. I do not miss it. I am told that is the same as not losing it."

**KIT VEY** (Juno's brother, 15). Scouts ahead with a kite-drone and a stripped buggy. Loud, funny, recklessly brave.
> "Kite's up! Good news: I can see the Dredge. Bad news: I can see the Dredge."
> "The fever winter? You sang the bridge song all night. ...Jun? Come on. You remember."

**OMA DESH** (the Wake's elder and mechanic). She runs **the Bench**, the workshop crawler (the garage). Gruff and thrifty, never sentimental out loud.
> "Everything on this rig was something else first. That's not salvage, girl. That's family history."
> "You can have the condenser or the cannon. Not both, not neither. Pick."

**THE FOREMAN** (Augustin Pell, the antagonist). Heard only on open channels: patient, reasonable, never shouting.
> "Do you know what a day costs on Gaunt, little rig? Ten years of walking. Nobody gets to sit down."
> "Work is the only prayer that gets answered." (Always *word for word*. A clue.)

**TALLOW** (a Dredge ace and a ghost). A pressed mind fraying into loops. Unsettling at first, sad later.
> "Hello hello. You smell like me. Like cold. Hello hello."
> "I had a name. It was a short one. It was a— it was a short one."

## The Throughline

Act one is a road movie in which a guarded girl and an earnest machine bond. In act two the scarcity bites and every Bench choice costs something. In act three the bond breaks on purpose, and the player rebuilds it knowing the truth.

**Juno's arc:** she has never stopped moving or carrying everyone, and she learns that letting go is not the same as losing. **Moth's arc:** a machine with an empty past fills itself with one person, then has to decide whether loving her means keeping her or freeing her.

---

## The Twist

**Juno died in Level 1.** When the shelf collapses, the cockpit floods. Moth was built as a **Cantor**, a frame made to carry the minds of the dying, so it pulls her into its lattice. She has been piloting as a ghost ever since. Moments before the ice broke she made Moth promise: *"Don't let me end up one of his ghosts."* So Moth lied. It told her she survived. It also told her the memory fragments filling its lattice were scraps of its own forgotten past.

**Those fragments are hers.** Every fragment the player overwrote at the Bench to run stronger parts was a piece of Juno: her father's hands on a steering bar, the bridge song she sang to Kit, the name of her family's first rig.

In Level 6 the garage itself delivers the reveal: the lattice header retypes, letter by letter, from `MOTH — RECOVERED FRAGMENTS` to `JUNO VEY`.

**A second, smaller reveal comes in Level 7:** the Foreman is a ghost too, a ninety-year-old pressed mind decaying into loops. He has been hunting Moth because a Cantor lattice does not decay, and he wants to live in it.

## The Campaign: Eight Pushes Across Gaunt

Each level is one long, wide map, opening on an in-engine vista of the landmark ahead.

**1. THAW — The Rime Shelf.** A frozen sea at the night edge, under stars and a red dawn rim, with a colony freighter frozen upright in the ice. Raiders hit Juno and Kit while they cut ice, and Juno shelters in a frozen war-frame that wakes. *Set piece:* sunrise sweeps the shelf, the ice calves, Moth falls through and the vitals flatline, then a sprint across breaking floes. *Twist:* Moth "remembers" its systems mid-fight (boost, then lock-on, then blade), and advancing sunlight rots the ice underfoot.

**2. THE WAKE — The Morning Steppe.** Red-gold reed plains under a low sun, with turbine ruins and **the Sundial**, a kilometre-tall colony gnomon the Wake steers by. *Set piece:* Dredge land-yachts harpoon a water tanker, and Juno cuts it free at full boost. *Twist:* the level *is* the convoy. Forty rigs keep rolling, the playable space moves with them, and any rig left behind is lost.

**3. THE THROAT — Gerrow Canyon.** Red slot canyons. The Foreman's **Gerrow Dam** banks a meltwater river above the bridge Juno grew up beside. *Set piece:* Juno blows the dam and rides the flood down to the Wake's empty tanks. *Twist:* the first half is a vertical climb on wall-boosts, the second a high-speed hover chase on the flood.

**4. AFTERNOON — The Glasslands.** To flank the Dredge, Juno crosses the burning afternoon: vitrified desert and the shattered **Parasol**, a fallen orbital mirror whose shards focus sunlight into sweeping burn-lines. *Set piece:* a duel with Tallow among the shards. *Twist:* heat economy. Sun and boosting heat the frame, and overheating locks the weapons, so Juno moves from shadow to shadow. *Choice:* tear out Tallow's core (her phase-dash) or leave her running.

**5. THE LONG NIGHT — Carrow Station.** A shortcut through total darkness under aurora, past frozen terraforming pylons to an abandoned pressing facility. *Set piece:* racked ghosts wake one by one as Juno passes, their cab lights blinking on. *Twist:* the headlamp and sonar drain power and give away her position. Juno escorts Kit's buggy and follows his flares. Few enemies, paced like horror.

**6. THE DREDGE — The Walking City.** A three-kilometre mining city on forty tracks, with bucket-wheels, flare-stacks and canyons of conveyors. *Set piece:* Juno boards at the tail and climbs to **the Press** at its heart, where the Foreman shows her her own registration and Moth confesses. *Twist:* the ground is a machine that keeps reconfiguring. After the reveal, the Foreman's recall signal tries to pull Juno out of Moth: inputs drift and the screen tears until she leaps off the bow.

**7. THE BONEYARD — Founders' Field.** A colony-war battlefield of colossal dead walkers beneath a kilometre-high frozen waterfall. Moth was built here. *Set piece:* the Wake's last stand against the full Dredge fleet, at Supreme Commander scale. Moth "sings" dead Cantors awake for minutes at a time, and the Foreman's secret comes out. *Twist:* a command layer. A radial wheel directs Kit's kites, Oma's mortar rigs and the woken Cantors, and every part given to the Wake shows up as an asset. Whether Oma survives holding the gate depends on how well the Wake was armed.

**8. THE STILL — The Pole.** Endless golden twilight, the sun resting on the horizon, a calm sea, snow falling straight down. *Set piece:* Juno chases the Dredge across the ice, climbs it as it breaks apart, defeats the Foreman's towering frame, then descends into the Press, an abstract lattice-space of light. *Twist:* Moth cannot follow her in, so there is no Sync. Each fragment she still has becomes a shield pip (Retold ones count as half), and Tallow fights beside her if she was spared.

## The Bench: A Garage Without Money

Between levels the frame kneels in Oma's crawler. Nothing is bought. Every upgrade is paid for from something the player cares about.

**Haul.** A staggered enemy can be finished with a **tear-off** that rips out a named part: a harpoon (grapple-pull), a crane-claw (grab-and-throw), skate-jets (water hover), a flare pod. The rack holds three, so the player chooses in the field what to bring home.

**Three spends:**
- **Steel: install it.** Six hardpoints (two arms, two shoulders, legs, core). Each part changes how the mech plays, not just its numbers.
- **Mind: feed it.** The best parts are *hungry*: running one permanently overwrites one of twelve **fragments**, short memory cards like "Rain on a tin roof," "A small hand on a kite string" or "The first rig was called Patience." The number of intact fragments sets the size of the **Sync** meter, which powers Moth's partner moves: *Moth Drives* (Moth steers and dodges while you aim), *Second Sight* (predicted missile paths) and *Hold* (survive one lethal hit).
- **Water: give it away.** Oma strips the part for the Wake. That adds water days, keeps names on the Wake Roll, or funds an asset the player can call in during missions (spotter kite, mortar rig, resupply trike).

Lost fragments never come back on their own.

**How it evolves:**
- **L1–2:** Plain parts only. Before Level 3 one overwrite is forced: the canyon needs the skate-jets, and Moth suggests "a bridge in the rain," calling it "the least important."
- **L3–5:** Excellent hungry parts arrive while water drops every level. Moth calls the fragments "noise from an old life."
- **L6:** The header retypes to `JUNO VEY`. From then on Juno must type a confirmation for every overwrite. Moth offers a fourth spend: *"Take me instead."* Each of its four kernel cells spent makes its comms shorter and flatter. At zero Moth is silent and the Sync moves trigger automatically.
- **L7:** **Retelling.** Kit or Oma can restore a lost memory as a *Retold* fragment, worth half value and rewritten in their own words. It costs water (the Wake stops for a night) and needs that person alive.
- **L8:** The final loadout. Kit runs the Bench if Oma fell.

## Endings

The Dredge can only be stopped from inside the Press, by a mind that stays. Juno chooses:

1. **"I'll stay."** Juno takes the Foreman's chair, walks the Dredge to the Still and opens it as a home for the Wake. Moth walks out free, carrying what is left of her to Kit.
2. **"Moth stays."** Moth drives the Dredge into the polar sea. Juno keeps the frame and leads the Wake into the Still, a ghost who will slowly loop the way Tallow did. In the final scene, Kit reads her fragments back to her.
3. **"Nobody stays."** Juno and Moth shut the Press down together and release every pressed mind, themselves included. The frame kneels facing north, Kit ties his kite to its shoulder, and the Wake finally stops walking.

All three are bittersweet, and campaign state colours each: **the Wake Roll** (how many named people reach the Still), **which fragments survive** (if "the bridge song" is intact Juno sings it; if not, Kit sings it to her), and whether Oma and Tallow lived.

## Telling It Without Voice or Cutscenes

- **Comms with a typographic voice.** The prototype's typewriter comms, with a style per speaker: Moth in monospace small caps, the Foreman in calm italics, Tallow with glitched repeats, Kit in rushed bursts.
- **The HUD and the mech.** The frozen 60 bpm, the lattice header retyping, the input drift during the recall, Moth turning north when idle.
- **Scripted world events.** The shelf cracking, the dam bursting, the Dredge's plume growing on every horizon, all shown with in-engine camera moves and letterboxing.
- **Environmental storytelling.** Abandoned rigs with painted names, roadside cairns, readable logs at Carrow, all on canvas-generated textures.
- **Bench briefings and interstitials.** Oma briefs over the kneeling frame. Between levels a "Morning Count" card shows water days, the Wake Roll, one line of Juno's log, and a map of Gaunt with the dawn line creeping west.

## Build Notes

- **No human models, and the story justifies it.** People appear only as cab lights and distant silhouettes. Juno never leaves the cockpit because she can't. Everything on screen is machinery built from the prototype's bevelled plate kit.
- **Light is the art budget.** Each level has its own sun (red night-rim, morning gold, white afternoon, aurora dark, polar sunset), which is cheap to build and works well with bloom.
- **Big maps** are wide heightfield corridors streamed in chunks, with instanced rocks and ruins and one hero landmark each. On Low: impostor landmarks, shorter draw distance, no shadows.
- **Risks:** the moving levels (L2's convoy, L6's Dredge) need moving-platform physics in a local reference frame, so prototype them first. Keep the L7 command wheel small: three unit types and four orders.
