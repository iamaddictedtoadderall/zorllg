# LANTHORN: Pitch B, Identity and Consciousness

> **Build team only. Full spoilers. Never show the player.**

**Logline:** When the orbital relay carrying her mind shatters mid-battle, a stranded war-frame pilot must walk a broken continent home, guided by a voice on a dying channel and trading pieces of herself for the strength to get there.

**Tone:** It opens at StarCraft scale, then the lights go out and it becomes SOMA: a lonely, slow-burn walk through a world that is quietly *wrong*. The back half is Mad Max salvage country with Titanfall-sized set pieces. SOMA asks "are you the copy?" LANTHORN asks *would you notice if someone you loved had been replaced?*

## 1. The Truth (hidden from the player)

- **Casting.** On this ringed colony world, pilots lie in a **Cradle** at home and are "cast" into war frames over the **Line**, the ring of orbital relays in the sky. When a frame dies, its pilot "snaps back" home. War is cheap; pilots brag about how often they've "landed."
- **The lie.** Casting is not a loan. The cast mind runs in the Ring, and snap-back is a fresh wake from your *pre-cast scan*. Every frame death ended a mind.
- **The break.** In the war's final offensive, the Kesh Directorate destroys the Line. Every mind in the Ring dies; every body at home wakes from its scan. Only **anchored** frames, with an experimental local core, keep their pilots. Compact pilot Teodora Saric, callsign **TAPER**, flies one. Her original wakes at home that afternoon and lives a whole life.
- **Dormancy.** An anchored frame heals by going dormant and regrowing from salvage. At low power, this takes *years*. With no Ring, the clock cannot sync, so TAPER experiences each dormancy as one night.
- **Drift.** Stranded minds ("residuals") that learn how much time has passed tend to collapse. That's why the old pilot rule exists: *never tell a stranded pilot the date.*

| Level | Year | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | The war. Teo is 31; her daughter Ama is 2. |
| 2–3 | 14–15 | The war ended long ago, but machines still fight it. Teo's original finds a faint signal. Ama, 16, takes the mic as "Lark." |
| 4 | 21 | Ama, 23, has a baby, Nell. |
| 5–8 | 40 | Ama died two years ago. Nell, 19, has been Lark since, imitating her mother. Teo's original is 71, waiting. |

Nobody is secretly a clone. The copy question is raised openly in L4. The twists are about **time** and **who is on the radio**.

## 2. Cast

**TAPER (Teodora Saric).** The player: terse, dry, protective, funny under fire. She believes she'll snap back into her body at Lanthorn's Cradle.
> "Copy. Walking."
>
> "Tell me something that isn't about the war, Lark. Anything."

**Lark (Ama), L2–4.** The relay operator who finds her. Lowercase, quick, warm, dodges personal questions. Signs off "light's on."
> "ok good news the storm's moving east. bad news so are you. light's on"
>
> "don't worry about what day it is. days are overrated. focus on the giant walking robot part"

**Lark (Nell), L5–8.** Same callsign, a different person, and the player is not told. She capitalizes carefully and tries too hard to sound like her mother.
> "The bridge is clear. I think. Light's on."
>
> "Gran says you should rest. My mom. My mom says. Long day."

**KEEL.** The frame's maintenance mind and the garage's "mechanic." Literal, all caps. It absorbs every memory TAPER gives up, growing more human as she grows less.
> "INTEGRITY 41%. LEFT KNEE ACTUATOR STIFF. CEDE ONE (1) MEMORY TO RESTORE BOOST."
>
> "REPAIR COMPLETE. IT IS RAINING. YOU LIKE THE RAIN. I DO NOT KNOW HOW I KNOW THAT."

**HARROW.** Kesh ace, anchored like TAPER. A vain duelist in L1; later a chimera frame carrying thirty grafted minds whose echoes break into his lines.
> "Compact pilots die so cheaply. Let me show you one who costs something."
>
> "I went home, Taper. [home] Someone was wearing my face. [our face] [my face]"

**Captain Ekene Oduya (ANVIL).** TAPER's L1 commander: gruff, kind, made of rules. Cut off mid-sentence when the Ring breaks; returns in L5 as a captive core who still thinks it's Year 0.
> "Rule one if you're ever stranded: don't ask the date. Rule two: see rule one."
>
> "Taper? Where'd the Ring go? ...What's the date, Taper?"

**The Old Woman (Teo's original).** Two unsigned messages before the finale. Full stops on every line.
> "Left knee sticks in the cold. Tap it twice."
>
> "I left the light on. I didn't know what else to do."

## 3. The Campaign

Each level is one huge continuous map crossed edge to edge. The Ring overhead is the clock: crisp in L1, shattered and raining debris in L2, a faint dust band by L5. The score sheds instruments as it fades.

### L1. The Glass Front (Year 0)
- **Place:** Vitrified plains rising to the **Kesh Spire**, a black arcology on a plateau.
- **Set piece:** Hundreds of Compact frames, dropships and orbital lances push on the Spire's shield wall. TAPER duels Harrow at the gate. Then the Ring breaks: station lights die across the sky in a ripple, and every frame on the field drops mid-stride. Silence, then debris.
- **Twist:** Death is cheap: a **LANDINGS** counter respawns you instantly. After the break it reads **CAST LINK: NONE** forever.

### L2. The Saltreach (Year 14)
- **Place:** A dried inland sea: salt pans, dead turbines, a beached Kesh land-carrier the size of a town.
- **Set piece:** A whiteout salt storm. Feral walkers hunt by sound while the carrier's guns wake one by one.
- **Twist:** Power starvation. TAPER drains wrecks and solar fields to boost or fire. Visibility and lock range shrink.
- **Clues:** First contact ("hello? is someone in there?"). The clock reads UNSYNCED, "fresh" wrecks are salt-crusted, and Lark won't say the date.

### L3. The Foundry That Never Stopped (Year 15)
- **Place:** An automated Kesh forge in a volcanic rift: lava canals, kilometre-long conveyor bridges, cranes assembling walkers.
- **Set piece:** Climbing into the central forge while it builds enemies in real time, then killing its Overseer.
- **Twist:** A light RTS layer. Enemies roll off visible production lines; destroying specific fabricators changes what spawns.
- **Clues:** The Overseer loops "Quota cycle 5,4▒▒ exceeded." No pilots anywhere. An unsigned knee tip arrives: "my mom. she used to fly."

### L4. The Listening Fields (Year 21)
- **Place:** Highland meadows. A forest of giant antenna dishes stands around a Kesh Cradle complex, drowned in tall grass.
- **Set piece:** Night among the dishes. Harrow returns as a chimera stitched from other frames. He loses the fight but cripples TAPER's frame, forcing the longest dormancy.
- **Twist:** Signal warfare. Tune dishes to lure, jam or stun feral cores; hide in the grass.
- **Story and clues:** Terminal logs reveal snap-back and drift; TAPER's body may already be awake. An L1 frame stands with a tree grown through its chest. Lark: "the little one's teething." Harrow: "Ask her what year it is, Taper."

### L5. The Procession (Year 40)
- **Place:** Canyon country cut by a ruined highway that ends at a megabridge.
- **Set piece:** A 15 km running battle along the **Procession**, a salvager land-train hauling captive war cores. It ends as the bridge collapses.
- **Twist:** A moving battlefield: fight across and between vehicles; stopping means losing the train. Captive cores whisper on open comms, Oduya among them.
- **Clues:** Weathered layers of candle graffiti. A salvager says: "the Candle-walker. My grandfather saw you." Lark now capitalizes, and gets TAPER's dog's name wrong.
- **Reveal:** A salvager uplink syncs the clock. The HUD date spins from DAY 9 past DAY 14,600 as the camera pulls away from a frame standing very still. *"Lark." / "I know." / "How long." / "Forty years. I'm sorry. Light's on."*

### L6. Ringfall (Year 40)
- **Place:** A snow-buried pine forest. A fallen ring station lies across the valley like a mountain range.
- **Set piece:** Climbing through Harrow's lair inside the wreck. He cuts Lark's channel and argues the whole way that residuals are the *continuous* selves, and those who woke from older scans are the real copies. His graft attack floods the HUD with voices. TAPER escapes, but he copies her command codes.
- **Twist:** Gravity plates still working in patches create sideways and low-gravity pockets for long hover arcs. "Chorus intrusions" briefly seize her weapons or steering.
- **Stakes:** Harrow marches on Lanthorn, home of the last working Cradle, to write his chorus into living bodies.

### L7. The Lighthouse (Year 40)
- **Place:** A storm coast of black cliffs. Lark's relay is built into an old lighthouse at Cass Point, a place from one of TAPER's memory cards.
- **Set piece:** Holding the station against Harrow's raiders in a lightning storm, 20 m waves breaking over the causeway.
- **Twist:** Defend, don't advance. Lightning charges whatever conducts it, TAPER included, and the tide reshapes the map every few minutes.
- **Reveal:** Under fire, Nell tells the truth: until two years ago, Lark was Ama, TAPER's daughter. TAPER's floodlight finds forty years of logs on the walls, height marks on a doorframe, a candle carved on a grave. If "Ama asleep on my chest" survived the garage, TAPER says *"She was two."* If it was ceded, she says *"I don't know that name."* Nell answers: *"...She knew yours."*

### L8. Lanthorn (Year 40)
- **Place:** TAPER's home city in heavy rain, half rebuilt inside salvage walls around the Cradle dome.
- **Set piece:** Harrow's chorus army storms the walls. TAPER fights beside militia walkers built from old Compact plates in a Supreme Commander-scale battle, ending in a duel on the dome in lightning.
- **Twist:** The garage picks your allies: released cores return piloting militia walkers; grafted ones fight from inside you.
- **Finale:** A small figure with a lantern walks out into the rain to face a twelve-metre machine.

## 4. Twists and Clue Placement

1. **Forty years** (end of L5).
2. **Lark is family, and Lark changed** (L7). The real knife: the voice was TAPER's daughter, who died between L4 and L5, and the player never noticed the handoff. It is snap-back horror in plain sight.
3. **Keel remembers** (finale, conditional). Ceded memories went into Keel. Heavy ceders learn the machine remembers Ama and TAPER does not.

| Clue | Where | Pays off |
|---|---|---|
| Oduya's "don't ask the date" joke, the Ring clock, UNSYNCED, overgrowth, the quota counter, Harrow's taunt | L1–L4 | Twist 1 |
| Memory card: *"Leave the light on for when Mama comes back"* | Dormancy 1 | Lark's sign-off, Twist 2 |
| Unsigned knee tip; "my mom used to fly" | L3 | The Old Woman |
| "The little one's teething" | L4 | Nell exists |
| New capitals, wrong dog name, the "Gran" slip | L5–6 | The handoff |
| Keel knows TAPER likes rain | Dormancy 4+ | Twist 3 |

After the credits, an **annotated comms log** unlocks: every Lark message re-dated and signed Ama or Nell.

## 5. The Garage: Dormancy

Between levels the frame kneels where it stopped and goes dark to repair; Keel runs the screen. There is no money. The currency is **substrate**: the core is fixed-size, TAPER's mind fills nearly all of it, and every module needs room to run.

**Salvage** from wrecks repairs armour and builds hardware: rifles, blades, missile racks, shields, boost kits, and frame posture (light, medium or heavy, reusing the Striker, Vanguard and Bastion silhouettes). Hardware is dead weight until substrate runs it, and substrate comes three ways:

1. **Cede a memory.** Burn one of TAPER's ~18 vignette cards ("Rain on the hangar roof," "Ama asleep on my chest"). The module runs perfectly, but the memory is gone: interstitials show redaction bars, some comms replies vanish, and Keel grows. Memories formed during play ("Lark's laugh") become cards too.
2. **Graft an engram** from a defeated anchored core (feral aces, Harrow's chorus, the Procession's captives). Each adds a skill: a duelist's blade chain, a spotter's lock range, an extra quick-boost charge. The cost is **bleed**: intrusive comms lines, HUD ghosts, aim drifting toward targets *they* hated. Their memories slip into your card list behind a source tag that fades as Chorus rises, so late on you may cede a stranger's memory believing it yours, or the reverse.
3. **Release** a graft by ejecting its core at a salvager waystation. You lose the skill and stop the bleed. Some grafts beg to stay. Released minds return in L8.

Two meters result: **Integrity** (how much of the core is still TAPER) and **Chorus** (how much is borrowed). Neither is "good." Both change how the frame plays and which endings exist.

**How it evolves:** Dormancy 1 allows ceding only; restoring boost costs one memory, the first real choice. Single feral grafts appear in Dormancies 2–3. By Dormancy 4 the Kesh logs have explained bleed and drift. Dormancy 5 offers a dozen captive cores, Oduya among them: grafting him adds a tactical overlay and a voice that keeps asking the date. After the reveal, dormancies last hours and show real dates, and ceding hurts more because the player knows who Ama is.

## 6. Endings

When Harrow falls, the Old Woman offers TAPER her own body through the Cradle: *"I've had forty years. You've had a walk. Take the rest."* The choice is real, and the garage decides which options exist.

- **Come Home** (always available). TAPER overwrites her original. Epilogue cards: rain on skin, Nell's hand, songs she doesn't know.
- **Become One** (Integrity ≥ 50%). A lossy merge produces one old woman who remembers both the life and the walk. She goes to the lighthouse every night.
- **Keep the Light** (always available). TAPER stays a machine and walks back out to find the other residuals and gently tell them the date, becoming someone else's Lark. With high Chorus she says "we": a kinder Harrow.
- **Keel** (Integrity < 25%). The Cradle finds "not enough of you to come home." The Old Woman speaks to the frame, and Keel answers with the memories TAPER gave away.

## 7. Telling It Without Voices or Cutscenes

- **Comms:** typewriter text, a signal bar per channel, typography per character (Lark's lowercase, Keel's caps, Harrow's brackets). Control is rarely taken away.
- **Scripted world events:** the army dropping, the date spin, the lighthouse window, the lantern in the rain.
- **Environment:** the Ring clock, overgrowth, layered graffiti, callsigned wrecks, three-line terminal logs.
- **Briefings and interstitials:** Lark's route notes on a canvas-drawn map, plus one card of TAPER's thoughts before each sleep, redacted where memories were ceded.
- **The HUD:** LANDINGS, UNSYNCED, Integrity and Chorus are story.

## 8. Buildability

- **One human figure**: the Old Woman, a low-poly silhouette with a lantern, seen from frame height.
- **Machines are the cast.** Walkers, chimeras, salvager rigs, land-trains and dropships all come from **one bevelled-plate library**. Landmarks (Spire, carrier, dishes, fallen station, lighthouse, dome) are large extruded bevelled profiles.
- **Weather does the heavy lifting.** Salt, ash, snow, spray, rain and lightning are reusable particle, fog and bloom presets that Low thins out.
- **Maps** are 2–4 km corridors streamed in chunks. The L1 army is instanced low-detail frames sharing one "drop" animation.
