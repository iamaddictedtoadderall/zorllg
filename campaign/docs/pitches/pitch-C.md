# BANKED FIRE: Pitch C, War at Scale

> **Build team only. Full spoilers. Never show the player.**

**Logline:** Sixty years into an ash-black winter, a Warden mech pilot deserts mid-battle in a three-way war over the last volcanic Kilns, builds a coalition of enemies out of nothing but kept promises, and climbs above the ash to learn why this war never ends.

**Pillars:** StarCraft 2 and Supreme Commander 2 scale, Mad Max scarcity, and a SOMA-grade question at the end. The player is always one machine inside a battle far bigger than them.

## The World

**The Calder Reach**, 61 years after **the Dimming**, when old-world magma taps cracked the Calderline rift and filled the sky with ash. The sun is a rumour. The only heat left comes from the **Kilns**, geothermal stacks along the rift that warm terraced glasshouses. There are not enough Kilns, and nobody says the number.

| Faction | Creed | War machines | Look |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wardens** (southern Kilns) | "Warmth is earned." Heat rationed by labour. | Walker lines, artillery crawlers, searchlight towers | Graphite, amber lamps, hard bevels |
| **Cinder Kin** (the ash flats) | "Fire belongs to whoever can carry it." | Sail-hulks on tracks, skimmers, harpoon walkers, flame lances | Rust, chrome, painted flames, riveted plate |
| **Clear Synod** (northern basin) | "One winter's sacrifice for a thousand summers." | Ceramic drones, shield pylons, beam Lancers | White glaze, cyan seams, soft chamfers |

On the summit of Mount Meridian, above the ash, stands **the Lance**, an old-world igniter built to burn the sky clean. The Synod swears it will be finished "in five winters."

## Cast

**Captain Noor Asante, callsign FLINT (the player).** A Warden pilot raised by the Marshal after her parents froze. Terse and formal, until that cracks.
> "Flint to Line. Terraces clear. Bring the count up from the low gardens."
> "You taught me to read, Marshal. Then you put my name on a list."

**Marshal Oren Hask.** Warden commander and FLINT's foster father. Clipped; he talks in heat-hours and asks for exact numbers after every fight.
> "Line holds. Good. Now count them, Flint. I want the number before the ash settles."
> "Forty-one heat-hours a head this winter. You think I enjoy arithmetic?"

**Saint Gideon Scald.** Kin warlord-preacher. Roaring and joyful, he quotes his own scripture, the Burning Book, and calls everyone "kindling."
> "SING IT, KINDLING! Nobody freezes who dies on fire!"
> "Right on time, Warden girl. Come warm your hands. We kept you a seat."

**Provost Ilse Quell.** Head of the Synod. Courteous, she uses no contractions, speaks in numbered points and signs off "Clear skies."
> "Three points, Captain. One: your offer is noted. Two: it is insufficient. Three: clear skies."
> "The number has been reached. Synod forces will withdraw. This is not cruelty. It is arithmetic."

**Rhetta Saw, callsign GUTTER.** Kin champion pilot, FLINT's rival in L1 and wingmate from L3. Fast lowercase, Kin slang, total faith in Scald.
> "oi, cold-blood! you dance pretty for a ration-kid. do it again so i can shoot you proper"
> "he said we all go up bright. never said he was the one picking who"

**Tock (Marit Tocheva).** The Kin crew chief who rebuilds FLINT's mech: an ex-Synod engineer, warm and dry, who thinks in parentheses. She designed the Lance.
> "Hold still, love. Every bolt on you is somebody's promise. (Mind the left knee. It's Kin. It sulks.)"
> "I drew that tower with a pencil I stole. It works. That's always been the trouble with it."

## The Throughline

FLINT believes warmth is earned by obedience, and learns that the people who decide who stays warm have lied to everyone, out of something that looks like love. **The question:** what do you owe the people who kept you warm, once you learn what it cost? Gutter mirrors this from the Kin side. Tock carries the guilt of building the one way out and running from it.

## The Twist

**There is no war. There is a cull.**

Twenty-two years ago, after the Hunger Winter, Hask, Scald and Quell met above the ash at the Meridian as **the Table** and did the sum. The Kilns can warm about 410,000 people, and the Reach holds 470,000. They chose soldiers over children. Every year since, on **the Quiet Night** (when no army fights, supposedly from superstition), they set the season's war: which Kilns change hands and how many die on each side. Their guard, **the Stewards** (unmarked frames in sun-bleached gold), referee and count.

Every alliance and betrayal the player lives through was on the agenda, including FLINT's defection. Hask gave her the one order he knew she would refuse, trading her to Scald to keep her off the Escarpment line, where Warden losses were scheduled to be heaviest. **His betrayal and his love are the same act.** The Lance is real and works. The Table shelved it because firing it would take every Kiln's output for one winter.

Players expect a single traitor, and the game hands them one when the Synod abandons the Kin in L5. The real answer is that the three enemies are one government, and the player's rebellion was scheduled.

### Clue Plan

- **The title.** To bank a fire is to bury it in ash so it lasts the night. Tock says it in L4: "Oldest trick there is. Nobody gets warm, mind."
- **The bell.** A flare goes up and the winning side withdraws, in L1, L2 and L5.
- **Shared words.** Hask and Quell both say "arithmetic"; Scald preaches "the sum."
- **The Watcher.** An unmarked gold frame watches from ridges in L1, L2, L4 and L5, and never fires.
- **Winter Count.** Interstitials list each season's dead per faction. Against the populations on L1's ration boards, all three come to the same 4%.
- **The banner.** In L3, minutes after FLINT defects, a sun-faded Kin banner reading FLINT is already hanging.
- **The lift.** The cable lift up Mount Meridian is on every skyline, and its cars move only on the Quiet Night.
- **Five winters.** L4 inscriptions dated twenty years back promise the Lance "in five winters."

## The Campaign: Eight Pushes Across the Reach

Each level is one large streamed map crossed start to end, in dusk colours until the end of L6.

**1. THE GLASS TERRACES (Kiln Seven).** *Place:* a volcano terraced with glowing glasshouses under a 2 km Kiln stack. *Set piece:* Kin sail-hulks ram the lower terraces under Warden artillery. FLINT duels Gutter on a collapsing aqueduct, and as the Kin break through, the bell flare sends them home. *Twist:* **the Line.** FLINT advances inside a formation of dozens of walkers. Holding formation gives her artillery and shield-wall cover; breaking ranks is faster but lonely.

**2. THE ASH SEA (Drowned Varrow).** *Place:* a city buried to the fortieth floor in ash dunes. Tower tops are islands, and Kin fleets sail between them. *Set piece:* a Warden raid on the Kin fire-stores ends when the leaning Needle tower falls across the fleeing hulks. The flagship's hold carries only families asleep around one stolen ember. *Twist:* **quickash.** A mech that stops on the dunes sinks, so momentum and rooftop-hopping are the core loop.

**3. SCALDHAVEN (the Fallen Colossus).** *Place:* the Kin capital, built inside a 400 m old-world siege machine lying on its side in a white canyon. *Set piece:* Hask orders FLINT to drop the Colossus's ribs on the shelter decks. She refuses. *Twist:* **the IFF flip.** Mid-level the HUD inverts from amber to ember and every ally becomes a target. The player re-crosses the map in reverse, with the Kin, against the Wardens.

**4. THE FUMAROLE GARDENS (Synod Basilica).** *Place:* white travertine terraces and hot springs around the Basilica, a cathedral of ceramic pipes played like an organ to vent the Kiln. *Set piece:* FLINT helps hold the Basilica against the Wardens until the Organ sounds a full chord that blows their walkers off the terraces. Quell agrees to an alliance. *Twist:* **the chords.** Vents erupt in time with the procedural score, steam leaves only thermal sight, and chained shield domes must be cut pylon by pylon.

**5. THE ESCARPMENT (the Warden Wall).** *Place:* a 300 m basalt cliff topped by a fortress and the great gun Magistrate, above a plain holding three armies: the game's biggest battle. *Set piece:* the Kin and Synod assault under a Synod shield umbrella. At the breach Quell says, "The number has been reached." The umbrella drops, and FLINT holds the breach while the Kin retreat. *Twist:* **Banners.** FLINT plants banners to command allied squads.

**6. THE QUIET NIGHT (Mount Meridian).** *Place:* a mountain of black basalt columns in a lightning storm. *Set piece:* FLINT and Gutter follow the lift cars up through the ash ceiling into the game's first sunlight, with the Lance above a sea of cloud. On an open channel, three familiar voices calmly set next season's dead, FLINT's company among them. The Stewards find them, and the level ends with a fall back into the dark. *Twist:* **the truce.** Firing a weapon wakes every army on the mountain, so the level is climbing, weather and hiding.

**7. KILN ZERO (the Caldera Sluices).** *Place:* a caldera around a lava lake, with old-world sluice gates feeding the Kilns. *Set piece:* at the scheduled spring battle, FLINT broadcasts the Table's agenda and opens the sluices. Lava cuts the Stewards' lines as three armies turn their guns the same way for the first time. *Twist:* **the Script.** The HUD shows the Table's timeline ("12:44 Warden barrage. Expected: 600."), and every beat the player breaks turns that unit into mutineers who join FLINT. Gutter's unit is due to die at 12:44; she lives only if the player kills the spotters first. Tock hands over the Lance's ignition codes.

**8. ABOVE THE ASH (the Meridian).** *Place:* a sunlit summit, the Lance's mirror spiral over an ocean of cloud: the game's only true colour. *Set piece:* FLINT fights up the charging Lance against the commanders' personal frames: Hask's artillery, Scald's speed and fire, Quell's shields and beams. If Gutter lives and Kin Trust is high, Scald turns on the others: "I'll not die at a TABLE. Give me a FIRE." *Twist:* **your army.** Which allies ride the lift behind FLINT depends entirely on the promises she kept.

## Sworn Steel: The Garage

The Reach has no money. **Every part is a promise.**

**The frame.** Six hardpoints (core, legs, two arms, shoulders, back). Each faction's parts change how the mech plays:
- **Warden:** *Bastide* shoulders call a creeping barrage; *Picket* legs brace into a turret (no stagger, no hover).
- **Kin:** the *Gaff* harpoon arm reels an enemy in or pulls FLINT to terrain; the *Ash-sail* back rides wind lines across open ground; the *Firebrand* flame lance melts shields but builds heat.
- **Synod:** the *Halo* core absorbs a volley and returns it as a beam; *Kite* mirror drones catch missiles or bend beams around cover; *Stilt* legs give huge strides but are fragile.

**The rules.**
1. Parts are **sworn for**, not bought. Each carries a **Pledge**, a concrete promise such as "Bring three Kin fire-wagons through the Narrows" or "No flame inside a glasshouse." Pledges become optional objectives in the next mission.
2. **Word** caps how many pledges FLINT can hold: two at L3, five by L7. More parts means more promises to keep mid-fight.
3. **Keeping** a pledge raises that faction's **Trust** and ties a visible knot on the mech's banner cord. Trust unlocks better parts and **Musters**, allied squads that fight beside her.
4. **Breaking** one lets FLINT keep the part, but it becomes **Forsworn**: it cannot be upgraded, and that faction's comms and Musters cool.
5. Pledges conflict. The Kin want a relay burned that the Synod want guarded, so every loadout decides whom FLINT owes.
6. Returning a part before a mission releases its pledge with no penalty.

**How it evolves.**
- **L1–2, Requisition:** Warden parts come with orders, not pledges. No choice, no knots: the contrast is the point.
- **L3–5, the Forge:** Tock's hangar opens. Kin pledges come first, Synod ones from L4. The L5 betrayal voids open Synod pledges at no Trust cost.
- **L6–7, Soldiers' pledges:** mutineers from all three armies offer parts from their own frames for promises like "Bring my squad home," and fight beside FLINT if she keeps them.
- **L8, Table steel:** the Stewards' sunlit parts are the best in the game, and their pledges are atrocities ("Let the Kin spearhead fall"). Taking any of it quietly marks FLINT for the Seat.

At the Lance, Quell reads her record aloud: *"You kept fourteen of nineteen promises, Captain. We have kept every one of ours. To each other."*

## Endings

With the commanders beaten and the Lance primed, the Table offers FLINT a seat. By design there are two doors and no third.

- **Take the Seat.** FLINT banks the fire, and the cull goes on, run by someone who understands its cost. The epilogue is next season's Winter Count with a fourth column headed FLINT. Hask sends one line: "Count them."
- **Light the Lance.** Every Kiln's output pours into the sky for one black winter. Hask stays at the manual feed, and his last comm is "Count me." The epilogue depends on Trust:
  - **The Shared Winter** (high Trust with all three factions): Kin hulks carry Warden children to the Synod's steam gardens, and in spring blue sky opens over the L1 terraces. *In battle: 0. To cold: 31,000.*
  - **The Last War** (low Trust): the factions fight over the last heat, and the sun rises on a smaller world.

## Telling It Without Voice or Cutscenes

- **Typewriter comms with a style per speaker:** Hask in small caps, Scald in ember-red capitals, Quell in numbered points, Gutter in lowercase, Tock in parentheses. In L6 the player recognises three styles in one channel before reading a word.
- **Scripted world events** with in-engine camera moves: the bell flare, the Needle falling, the HUD inverting, the umbrella dropping, the break into sunlight.
- **Environmental storytelling** on canvas textures: ration boards, chalked tallies, Kin banners, the Watcher, the lift cars, the knots on FLINT's cord.
- **War-table briefings:** a miniature of the next map's heightfield with unit tokens, narrated over comms.
- **Interstitials:** the Winter Count card (a clue in plain sight) and a soldier's letter.

## Build Notes

- **No people on screen,** only lit windows and distant silhouettes. Everything else is built from the prototype's bevelled plate kit, with one profile language per faction: Warden slabs, Kin riveted asymmetry with sails, Synod rounded ceramic.
- **Scale in three tiers:** near units with full AI, instanced mid units with simple AI, and a far "battle painting" of silhouettes on splines, tracers and artillery arcs. Low thins the outer tiers.
- **Light is the art budget.** Dusk suits bloom, and the sunlight of L6 and L8 is the payoff. Maps are chunked heightfields with one hero landmark each.
- **Risks:** the pledge and objective framework (build it first), the L3 team swap, the L7 timeline, and L5's Banners (three squad types, four orders).
