# Pitch Judging

> Build team only. Contains spoilers for all three pitches.

## Scores (1 to 10)

| Criterion | A: DAWNWAKE | B: LANTHORN | C: BANKED FIRE |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Originality | 8 | 8 | 6 |
| Emotional hook | 9 | 9 | 7 |
| Strength and fairness of the twist | 9 | 9 | 7 |
| Variety of the 8 levels and their gimmicks | 9 | 8 | 9 |
| Told through text comms and in-engine events | 9 | 9 | 7 |
| Garage ties into story and changes gameplay | 10 | 8 | 9 |
| Buildability in a procedural browser engine | 7 | 6 | 4 |
| **Total (of 70)** | **61** | **57** | **49** |

### A: DAWNWAKE (61)
- **Originality 8.** The planet where the dawn moves 11 km a day is the most original world of the three, and every level's lighting comes straight out of it. "The pilot was dead all along" is a familiar twist. What's new is that the player's own garage choices are what hid it.
- **Emotional hook 9.** A sister, her little brother, a thirsty convoy, and a machine learning to be a person by watching her. The overwrite mechanic makes the player complicit in her forgetting.
- **Twist 9.** Fair and thoroughly clued: the fixed 60 bpm, "Weirdly fine" at 61°C, "She is resting", the bridge in the rain. The reveal arrives through a UI the player has used for five levels. The second reveal (the Foreman is a looping ghost) also fits.
- **Variety 9.** Rotting sea ice, a moving convoy, a climb followed by a flood chase, heat economy, horror in the dark, climbing a walking city, a command battle, and a mind-space finale. No two levels play alike.
- **Comms and in-engine 9.** The story justifies having no human models (Juno can't leave the cockpit). HUD widgets act as storytellers, and every speaker has a distinct typographic voice.
- **Garage 10.** The garage is how the twist is delivered. Tear-offs change what the mech can do, the Sync meter turns memories into abilities you can feel, and giving parts to the Wake turns them into lives saved.
- **Buildability 7.** No humans, and the art leans on lighting, which is cheap. Two levels need moving-world tech (convoy, walking city), and L7 needs a small RTS layer. Both can be made manageable (see the bible).

### B: LANTHORN (57)
- **Originality 8.** The radio voice that is quietly passed from daughter to granddaughter is the best single idea in any of the three pitches. The setting (ring relays, a generic Directorate) is weaker.
- **Emotional hook 9.** A mother walking home to a two-year-old who grows up and dies on the radio. "She was two" is devastating.
- **Twist 9.** Forty years plus the Lark handoff, clued through typography. There are three twists, so it sits slightly on the edge of over-layered.
- **Variety 8.** Strong, but power starvation (L2), signal warfare (L4) and the lightning defence (L7) all lean on the same "resource drains while you fight" idea.
- **Comms 9.** The twist literally lives in the comms typography.
- **Garage 8.** Cede, graft and release is rich, but it overlaps with A's memory-for-power idea. "Bleed" (aim drifting toward targets the graft hated) risks feeling like the game fighting the player.
- **Buildability 6.** A field army of hundreds plus the ring breaking (L1), a production-line RTS (L3), a 15 km land-train (L5), gravity pockets (L6), a tide that reshapes the map (L7), and a human figure in the finale.

### C: BANKED FIRE (49)
- **Originality 6.** "The three enemy leaders are secretly one government running a cull" is well-worn (1984, Metal Gear, Hunger Games). The ash-winter Kilns setting is strong.
- **Emotional hook 7.** Hask betraying her out of love is excellent, but the stakes are statistical (410,000 vs 470,000) and the plot is political rather than intimate.
- **Twist 7.** Well planted but over-clued. The bell flare, the shared word "arithmetic" and a banner already hanging give it away by L3.
- **Variety 9.** The most inventive list of gimmicks: formation, quickash, IFF flip, organ chords, banners, stealth truce, the Script.
- **Comms 7.** Great per-speaker styles, and the three-voices-in-one-channel moment works well. But alliances, negotiations and offscreen politics need more exposition than comms can comfortably carry.
- **Garage 9.** Pledges becoming optional objectives is the best gameplay link to a garage in any of the three.
- **Buildability 4.** Three full faction art kits, three-tier armies in most levels, an IFF swap, a scripted timeline system, and a pledge framework that needs many objective types. Biggest scope by far.

## Decision

**Spine: Pitch A, DAWNWAKE.** It has the highest total, and its garage is the delivery mechanism for its twist, which is exactly what a pilot-and-machine game should aim for.

**What was grafted in, and why each one improves A:**
- **From C, "the Script" (L7).** The Foreman is a looping ghost, so at Founders' Field he re-fights the Founders' War beat for beat, and Moth (built there) can predict him. This makes the second twist something you play, not just read.
- **From C, the Watcher.** Tallow watches from ridges in L2 to L3 and never fires, foreshadowing the L4 duel.
- **From C, build notes.** A three-tier battle scale (near AI, instanced mid, painted far) for L2, L7 and L8, and the Founders' look (white ceramic, cyan seams), adapted from the Synod.
- **From B, "Made" fragments.** New memories formed during the campaign join the lattice, so the player has new things to lose, and the fragment economy doesn't run dry by L5.
- **From B, garage-gated endings.** Kept as A's three endings, but two of them now require something to be left: enough of Juno for "I'll stay", and at least one of Moth's kernel cells for "Moth stays".
- **From B, the annotated log.** After the credits, the "Lattice Log" annotates every lie and evasion Moth told.
- **From B, typography as story.** Moth's comms visibly degrade as its kernel is spent, and fragment titles retype into Juno's own words after the reveal.

**Left out deliberately:** C's pledges (excellent, but a second objective economy on top of the Bench would be the mush we were told to avoid), B's Lark handoff (it would compete with A's twist), B's bleed and grafted minds, and C's IFF flip.

**New creative-director additions** (not in any pitch, detailed in the bible): Oma has known since the first night; Moth "releases ballast" (Juno's body) at the end of L1; the Cantor creed "Carry them home. Set them down" ties the theme to the endings; Moth's lost forty-second passenger, Ada Corran; Tallyman Rusk as an early living antagonist who comes back as a ghost; Kit's capture as the reason for L6; the Foreman's frozen age, "ninety-one"; L8 dismantles the Dredge from outside rather than repeating L6's climb; and the Dredge's motion is animated in place, so the full moving-platform physics A worried about isn't needed.
